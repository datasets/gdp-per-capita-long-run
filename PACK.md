## discord

**New dataset: GDP per capita over the long run**

Who caught up since 1950? Of the 145 countries with Maddison Project estimates for both 1950 and 2022, 63 had a lower GDP per person relative to the US in 2022 than in 1950.

A few raced ahead: South Korea went from 6.5% of the US level to 70.6%, and Taiwan from 9.6% to 90.9%. In 1950 Ghana was richer per person than South Korea (1,788 against 998, in 2011 international dollars); by 2022 South Korea was 9.7 times richer (41,321 against 4,242).

The dataset has Maddison Project Database 2023 (169 countries, back to 1820 and earlier, plus regional and world averages) and Penn World Table 11.0 (185 countries, 1950 to 2023) as tidy CSVs, with a note on why the two differ. Both CC BY 4.0. {link}

title: Since 1950, 63 of 145 countries have lost ground relative to the US in GDP per person (Maddison Project 2023, tidy CSV)

I work on DataHub. We packaged the Maddison Project Database 2023 (Bolt and van Zanden) as tidy CSVs: GDP per person in 2011 international dollars and population for 169 countries, back to 1820 and earlier, plus Maddison's regional and world averages. There is also a separate file from Penn World Table 11.0 (185 countries, 1950 to 2023), with a short note on why the two differ (one PPP anchor and growth rates versus chained ICP benchmarks) and why they should not be spliced.

One thing we computed: of the 145 countries with estimates for both 1950 and 2022, 63 had a lower GDP per person relative to the US in 2022 than in 1950. South Korea went from 6.5% of the US level to 70.6% and Taiwan from 9.6% to 90.9%, while Ghana, richer per person than South Korea in 1950, slipped from 11.7% to 7.3%.

Caveats: this is relative to the US, not absolute decline; Maddison's 1950 figures for many poor countries are rough; and the Maddison terms ask that original papers be cited if you chart the data. Happy to hear where the method could be better.
